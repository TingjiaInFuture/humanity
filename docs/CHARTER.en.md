# Humanity Charter · Candidate Principles v0.2

**Status: open draft. It does not represent any nation, organization, class, or “humanity as a whole.”**

This is not a finished declaration asking for signatures. It is a starting point meant to be challenged and amended in public.

## Preamble

Humanity is building increasingly capable artificial intelligence and automation systems.

If machines eventually perform much of our thinking, labor, creation, and administration, a long-standing social assumption may begin to fail:

> People must exchange their labor value for the resources and status required to live.

We do not know when AGI will arrive, what form it will take, or what institutions will follow.

This draft therefore does not claim to predict one inevitable future. It asks a more basic question:

> If a person is no longer more economically useful than a machine, do they still possess a status that cannot legitimately be taken away?

## Principle 1: Survival should not depend on economic usefulness

No person should lose the conditions of basic survival and dignity merely because they cannot compete with machines, are no longer demanded by a market, or cannot provide enough labor.

Open questions include: What counts as basic conditions? Is this a moral principle, a legal right, or a policy objective? How do we prevent systems of provision from becoming systems of control?

## Principle 2: The gains of automation should benefit society broadly

When AI and automation substantially increase productive capacity, the gains should not default to unlimited concentration among a small number of asset owners.

This principle does not yet prescribe universal dividends, public ownership, taxation, public compute, or any other specific mechanism.

## Principle 3: People retain a voice in civilization's goals

AI may provide analysis, prediction, and execution, but major value goals affecting large numbers of people should not be set solely by a small number of institutions or automated systems.

Open questions include: What qualifies as a major value goal? Should humans hold a final veto? What if a machine-generated plan is demonstrably better than human judgment?

## Principle 4: Everyone has cognitive and technological freedom

People should have a right to know when AI systems materially influence them, and a right to reject non-consensual personality manipulation, forced enhancement, or algorithmic governance they cannot meaningfully exit.

Open questions include: Where is the boundary between manipulation and ordinary persuasion? How should children and people lacking full capacity be treated? Should low-AI or no-AI spaces be protected?

## Principle 5: The charter itself must remain amendable by the public

No founder, maintainer, expert, company, government, or AI should possess permanent and unchallengeable authority to interpret this charter.

The drafting process must leave a public record and permit dissent, amendment, and the transfer of power.

## Current co-creation process

The current stage is intentionally simple:

1. Anyone can start a topic through the public website.
2. The backend synchronizes it to GitHub Discussions.
3. Anyone can propose an exact-text amendment through the public website.
4. The backend first creates a GitHub Issue.
5. If the old passage appears exactly once in the target file, the backend creates a branch and Pull Request automatically.
6. The backend never merges automatically; acceptance remains a public human decision.

This is not the final governance system. It is minimum infrastructure for testing whether real participation exists.
